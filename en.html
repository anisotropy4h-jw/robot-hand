Squeeze too hard and it crumples. Too soft and it drops.

An 8-DOF robot hand instrumented with fingertip force and slip sensors, designed to regulate grasp force through closed-loop feedback. Everything on this page was measured on the physical hardware, not in simulation.

The grasp window is narrower than it looks

force closure / 3-contact precision grasp

Every object has a force window, [F_slip, F_damage]. Grip below it and the object slides out; grip above it and the object is destroyed. A human hand finds that band without thinking about it. A robot needs a sensor that reports contact force in real time and a controller that feeds it back.

The mission is a precision grasp on a small paper cup with three fingertip contacts — thumb, index and middle. The empty paper cup was selected because it has a narrow operating range between slip and buckling. A power grasp was intentionally excluded because palm contact can mechanically constrain the object and reduce the role of slip-based feedback.

Signal chain

FSR 3ch + PVDF 4ch

The two sensors answer different questions. The FSR reports how hard the fingertip is pressing; the PVDF reports the moment it starts to slip. Their bandwidth requirements differ, so the paths are physically separate. The FSR is low bandwidth and is read precisely through a 16-bit ADS1115, while slip vibration is far faster and goes straight into the ESP32 internal ADC. The ring finger channel is kept off the object and serves as a noise reference, so structural vibration and servo reaction can be subtracted out.

Bench log

filmed on hardware · not simulation
01 · actuation

Eight axes on one serial bus

Two servos per finger, eight axes total, driven over a single serial bus. Each axis is calibrated individually, then a script replays the open and close sequence.

Verified — bus communication, per-axis offsets, repeatability.
Not yet — no force feedback. Position commands only.
02 · teleoperation

From hand tracking to hardware

Webcam hand tracking streams through dora-rs, gets retargeted to robot joint angles by a QP-based IK solver, and is sent to the hardware. MuJoCo serves as both renderer and kinematic model.

Verified — tracking, IK and hardware running end to end within usable latency.
Scope — a classical geometric pipeline, not a learned policy.
03 · force sensing

Three FSR channels, live

Pressing one fingertip at a time raises a peak on that channel alone. The baseline settles at 0 V, and light presses land in the 0.4 to 0.9 V range.

Verified — channel independence, no crosstalk, the full I²C chain.
Not yet — no conversion to newtons. Load-cell calibration curve in progress.
04 · slip sensing

PVDF slip transient

At the amplifier output, the moment the paper cup begins to slip produces a distinct burst above the noise floor.

Verified — slip events separate from the noise floor on a compliant substrate.
Not yet — threshold not quantified, false-positive rate not measured.
